REFERENCE ID: NBD-HYDRO-206
TOPIC: Lake connectivity and drainage GIS study intake

OBJECTIVE
Analyze catchment boundaries, terrain-driven flow paths, stormwater links, barriers and possible historical interconnections. A modeled drainage path is a hypothesis until validated against actual infrastructure, rainfall and site conditions.

INPUTS
Study area and lake inventory; DTM/DEM/DSM resolution and vertical datum; observed drainage and culvert/bridge invert levels; rainfall time series and intensities; stream/road/land-use layers; historical channels; encroachment information; field survey; relevant authority constraints.

POSSIBLE METHODS
DEM conditioning (with documented reasons), sink treatment, flow direction and accumulation, watershed delineation, stream network extraction, connection graph analysis, overland flow investigation, scenario comparison and targeted field checks.

RISKS
Road embankments, buried drains, retaining walls, gates and culverts can invalidate an unconditioned DEM-derived path. Urban stormwater flow is affected by infrastructure. Do not equate modeled natural routes with confirmed engineering feasibility or legal right-of-way.

OUTPUTS
Maps, GIS data layers, flow connectivity diagrams, candidate reconnection reaches, uncertainty inventory, field-validation priorities and an engineering assessment report. Detailed drainage design requires additional hydrologic/hydraulic criteria and approvals.

CASE
'Assess 120 urban lakes with DTM and lake polygons' - ask about rainfall, culverts/invert elevations, historical drainage, field checks, vertical accuracy, selection of design storms and acceptance criteria.
